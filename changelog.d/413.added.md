- **`overlaps` - the paired-measurement window as its own dataset** (contract
  53). A `comparability` row declares that two origins may be read across and
  cited its evidence in `overlap_ref`, which was free prose. A reference that
  is a sentence is not a reference: nothing can follow it, nothing can count
  it, and a client deciding whether to trust the figure had to parse English.
  The census now has columns - `dataset`, `field`, `origin_a`, `origin_b`,
  `paired_days`, `dropped_days`, `from_date`, `to_date`.
- **What it deliberately does NOT hold**: the median, the low and the high of
  the paired differences, which the proposal that named this dataset listed
  among its columns. Contract 52 put all three on the `comparability` row one
  contract earlier, held to each other by validation, and carrying them here
  as well would be one width stated in two datasets kept honest by a
  cross-dataset rule. `comparability` DECLARES and holds the statistics;
  `overlaps` COUNTS.
- **`dataset` is on the census and not on the declaration**, because a field
  name does not identify a dataset: `distance_km` is a column of both `daily`
  and `sessions`, so a census that did not say which readings it counted could
  not be followed back to them - the sentence's own defect one level down.
- **A window is EARNED.** Fewer than three paired days is refused, being the
  count below which the engine will not measure a window at all; and
  `paired_days + dropped_days` may not exceed the days the window spans,
  because a day either paired or was dropped and a window cannot hold more
  days than it has. What one line cannot do is re-count the readings, so that
  is a corpus control rather than a row check - a check that re-counted would
  let lines appended later invalidate lines already written.
- **Exactly one shape of evidence per declaration, never both.** A
  `comparable` or `offset` row owes either a counted window or the sentence,
  and carrying both is refused: `vera`'s `overlap_ref` read "101 run(s) both
  instruments recorded, 2030-01-06 to 2030-06-30", which is the census in
  English beside the census. The requirement moved out of `validate_record`,
  which sees one line and cannot tell which shape is present, into
  `overlap_evidence_problems`, which `vitai validate` runs over both datasets.
  `policy._earns_its_status` - the gate that decides whether a row may lift the
  instrument seam at all - now accepts either shape, and refuses a census that
  has not earned itself.
- After this change a reader can tell from data which declarations rest on a
  counted window and which rest on a sentence. The demo's scale and DEXA
  overlap on two same-day readings, below the floor, so its row keeps its
  sentence and writes no census - and that pairing is the point.
- **The write door is looser and it is stated rather than discovered.**
  `jsonl.append` validates one line, so `Vitai.append` / `vitai append` / the
  MCP `claim` tool now accept a `comparable`/`offset` row carrying neither
  census nor sentence, where before contract 53 they refused it. `vitai
  validate` catches it, and the seam stays refused meanwhile, so nothing is
  read across on the strength of it. The alternative was to make an append
  depend on the contents of a second file, which is an ordering rule between
  two writes rather than a check on one line.
- **An advisory where the two halves of the rule can differ on time.**
  `overlap_evidence_problems` asks whether a census exists;
  `policy.comparability` asks whether one was in force on the date being
  judged. Those were one question while the evidence was a sentence, because a
  sentence travels on the row. A declaration whose only census postdates it is
  legal, validate-green, and refused for every week before that census -
  `overlap_timing_advisories` names the date it starts being honoured.
- `vitai overlap --dataset --field --origin-a --origin-b`, `Vitai.overlap(...)`
  and an `overlap` MCP tool. `vitai calibrate` reports the window it would
  write and no longer emits an `overlap_ref` sentence.
