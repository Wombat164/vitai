- **A fourteenth persona, whose axis is the life of an instrument** (#405).
  `hana` exists because #405's fix had no fixture: `outage` measured cadence
  in valid time, its first live question was a false positive against a
  one-time archive import, and the shipped corpus contained no import to hold
  the fix shut.
- Her record carries four daily channels and three of them are quiet at the
  horizon, which is the whole point. `old-band` is an archive - 106 dates
  across seven months of valid time at ONE transaction day - and is silent
  because it never had a rhythm to break. `club-treadmill` stopped in February
  with `instruments.to_date` closed on the day of the last session, and is
  silent because the record says so. `chest-strap` stopped in April with its
  instrument row still open and nothing accounting for it, and is **the one
  question this record produces**.
- The archive is deliberately UNDECLARED. The record that produced the false
  positive had no `instruments.jsonl` at all, so a fixture where the declared
  layer catches everything would leave the transaction-time layer underneath
  it untested.
- The corpus control that asserted the engine asks NOTHING now asserts it asks
  EXACTLY this - both directions rather than every refusal. Reverting the
  engine to valid-time cadence makes `old-band` fire; opening the treadmill's
  `to_date` makes it fire. Each failure names itself.
- **The archive lives in its own stream, `daily.old-band.jsonl`**, because
  that is what a one-time import is: a second writer (#105). `recorded_at` is
  monotonic across a dataset's concatenated files, so an import in its own
  file must be stamped after the main file's last row - which is also the
  honest story, since she pulled the archive at the end of the block.
