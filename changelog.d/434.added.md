- The rollup's `## Coverage` section says how many days the record spans that
  carry no row at all. The count beside it says how many ROWS the record holds,
  and `daily: 84` over an 84-day block reads as 84 days of calendar - which on
  the shipped demo it was, 84 rows over 84 dates with zero gaps, which is why
  the number was never questioned. Across the personas it is not: `rachel` holds
  170 rows spanning 423 days, `tom` holds 6 spanning 1,469, and 2,787 days
  corpus-wide have no row. A client rendering the count as a stretch of calendar
  was right about the one fixture it calibrates on and wrong about every record
  that mattered.
- The span is the record's own extent - first dated row to last - and that is
  the one candidate of the three that invents nothing. A last-N-days window
  would need an N, and `over_days` in this same module already refuses to name
  one: the engine has no published basis for where thin begins, and this repo
  has been bitten twice by cutoffs it invented. A declared range would be
  honest and no record declares one.
- It cannot see a gap at either end, and that is what extent means rather than a
  defect it has: a record holds nothing before its first row. Said out loud
  because the number would otherwise read as "days the athlete did not log",
  which is a bigger claim than this makes.
- No explained-versus-silent split, and that is the one thing it must not
  become. Contract 51 attaches a reason to a FIELD on a row, and a day with no
  row has nothing to hang one on - so calling these days unexplained would be an
  accusation the record has no way to answer. A test holds the line to
  observation words only.
- Silent when the record is continuous, and the demo now has one day it is not:
  the 31st of May, inside the travel week the record already says the routine
  fell apart in. A fixture that cannot produce the line cannot prove a client
  renders it, which is #423 and #427's rule pointed at a row that is not there
  rather than a value that is not. The demo reads `daily: 83` over an 84-day
  span, and says so.
