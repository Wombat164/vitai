- **`outage` measured cadence on the wrong clock and its first live question
  was a false positive** (#405). A one-time archive import - pulled once to
  recover years of readings from a replaced device - looked like the most
  established channel in the record and then like one that had died. It had
  finished, not failed.
- **The rule now measures in transaction time.** `date` is when a reading
  happened; `recorded_at` is when the record learned it, and "has this source
  been contributing" was always a question about the second. A backfill fakes
  a perfect multi-year cadence in valid time, which is why every existing
  guard passed it through: it is not seen once and holds far more than an
  anecdote's history. On the right clock it is a single day and cannot be
  silent, because it never had a rhythm to break. Still no number anywhere -
  the comparison is against the longest gap the source has already shown.
- **A declared end now outranks the inference.** `instruments.to_date` has
  been in the schema since contract 45 and nothing consulted it, so a record
  could already say a device was used until a date while the asking channel
  went on asking why it stopped. `retired_origins` reads it, and the rule
  refuses on a closed instrument before measuring anything - which is the one
  case no structural test can reach, since an instrument imported several
  times and then abandoned still looks live on any clock.
- **Retired means every interval has closed, not any of them.** A persona in
  this repo's corpus carries two `watch` rows: an old watch closed in
  February and its replacement opened the day after, reporting under the same
  name. A rule reading "some row has a past `to_date`" would call that watch
  retired and silence every question about a channel in daily use.
- **A source that never says when it was written is no longer asked about.**
  Reading a transaction cadence off `date` is the mistake being removed, so a
  row with no `recorded_at` supports no cadence at all. The refusal is the
  safe direction: an unasked question costs less than a confident one about an
  import that finished years ago.
