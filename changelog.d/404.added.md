- `protocols` gains `controls` (contract 50): the conditions a protocol
  DECLARES it fixes, from a new closed vocabulary in
  `semantics/body_state.toml` - `bladder`, `bowel`, `fed`, `hydration`,
  `clothing`, `footwear`, `post_exertion`. A protocols row said WHICH procedure
  was followed and never what that procedure controls FOR, so a reading either
  named a protocol or did not and nothing downstream could say which conditions
  were left free.
- The vocabulary is closed and validated through an alias layer, unlike the
  protocol slug beside it, which stays open. A slug is the athlete's own name
  for their own procedure; a condition exists only to be compared, and an open
  vocabulary would give one record `bladder` and the next `bladder_full` with
  nothing able to tell they are one fact.
- No magnitude reaches the field or its registry, and none may be derived from
  it. It says which conditions are fixed and never what one is worth; the
  arithmetic that would consume it is #402's and is blocked on evidence no
  record holds yet.
- Absent and empty are different facts: null means nobody has said, and an
  empty list is refused because it would assert that a procedure somebody wrote
  down controls nothing. A condition not listed beside others is unstated
  rather than declared free, so there is no companion `free` list.
