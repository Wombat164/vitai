- **A word that names more than one measurand is published as a question, not
  as an answer** (#400). `pulse` was an alias of `avg_hr` - a session average,
  heart rate while training - so a consumer resolving "what was my pulse"
  answered about a measurand the record will not vouch for. Two clients had it
  on two different fields and neither marked the choice as a choice.
- **It was six words, not one, and `pulse` was not the worst.** `calories`
  resolved to what was EATEN while `session calories` resolves to what was
  BURNED; `fat` resolved to grams of dietary fat while `body fat` resolves to a
  body-composition percentage; `duration`, `heart rate` and `hr` were in the
  same state. All six now sit in `ambiguous_aliases()`, published in
  `vitai schema --json`, naming their candidate fields so a client refuses with
  both named instead of guessing.
- **The gate that should have caught this existed and could not see it.**
  `test_no_alias_collides_with_another_field` says "a word that routes to two
  fields routes to neither, and it would do so silently" and compared whole
  strings, so `pulse` beside `resting pulse` walked past it. It now compares
  a bare alias against the tail of every qualified one, on the PUBLISHED
  surface rather than the registry file.
- The qualified forms are untouched: `resting pulse`, `calories eaten` and
  `body fat` still resolve to one field each, so the athlete's question is
  still recognised in the form that has an answer.
- No contract move, and measured rather than asserted: the demo read model
  builds byte-identical (`aaca2ab9...`) before and after. The alias vocabulary
  is not contract-versioned - contract 54 shipped while eight aliases were
  moved out of the same registry under #350 with no bump.
