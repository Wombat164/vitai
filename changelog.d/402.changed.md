- **`comparability` can hold a range that runs one way** (#402, contract 52).
  `difference_lo` and `difference_hi` carry the lowest and highest paired
  difference observed over the overlap, on the same signed convention `bias`
  already uses. `bias` is a point and `spread` a width, so until now the row
  could say "they leaned this way by this much" and "they got this far apart"
  and could not say "further above than below".
- **The asymmetry used to survive only in a `note`.** `vera` is the first
  record anywhere with a measured cross-instrument overlap, and her row said
  `bias: 0.0, spread: 1.26` beside the English sentence "it parts one way
  only". The engine measured four numbers and the dataset stored two, so an
  athlete appending what `vitai calibrate` produced destroyed the shape of the
  finding on write.
- **Measured against her 101 paired runs, `bias` plus or minus half the
  `spread` is wrong in both directions at once.** It puts the low edge at
  -0.63 km where the deepest low reading is -0.03 and no observation at all
  falls below it, and cuts the high edge at +0.63 where twenty-one of the 101
  runs lie above, out to +1.23. Six hundred metres of disagreement that never
  happened, and the canopy runs that are the entire finding dropped.
- **THE ROW STILL EARNS NO BAND, and adding the columns does not move #372 one
  step.** Two independent reasons, either sufficient. `offset` does not lift
  the instrument seam - `policy.comparability` and `all_comparable` are
  untouched - and a row that may not be read across is not a row a band
  derives from, whatever columns it carries. And observed extrema are not a
  coverage interval: the minimum and maximum of 101 differences are the two
  most sample-dependent numbers in the set, they can only widen as days
  arrive, and they say nothing about the 102nd run. A consumer still may not
  draw a band from a comparability row alone. What changed is that the record
  can hold what was measured without losing half of it, and that a consumer
  halving the spread is now contradicted by the row rather than only by a
  docstring it never reads.
- **Nothing required, and the ends are optional beside `offset` on purpose.**
  Requiring them would force a writer who honestly knows a width and not its
  ends to invent the ends, which is the fabrication this dataset exists to
  refuse arriving through a validation rule - and it would invalidate every
  `offset` row written before contract 52. So the columns express a third
  state rather than a second: a width whose ends are recorded, a width whose
  ends are not, and no width at all.
- **The redundancy is made into a check rather than left as a convention.**
  `spread` and the two ends state one width twice. Both are kept - every
  existing reader wants `spread`, and `spread` alone cannot say which way the
  range runs - so validation holds them to each other: both ends or neither,
  in order, `spread` required beside them and equal to their difference, and
  `bias` inside them where it is present. Forbidden beside `not_comparable`
  for `bias` and `spread`'s reason, since nothing was measured to have a
  range.
- **Named for what they bound.** Under this schema's own `_lo`/`_hi`
  convention - `kg_lo` bounds `kg`, `target_hi` bounds `target` -
  `bias_lo`/`bias_hi` would have read as bounds on the BIAS, a confidence
  interval on a median and exactly the coverage claim #402 forbids inventing,
  and `spread_lo`/`spread_hi` as bounds on the WIDTH. These bound the
  DIFFERENCE, which names the row's implicit subject out loud for the first
  time: `bias` and `spread` were always statistics OF the difference and
  nothing on the row said so.
- The options that lost are written down rather than discarded, including the
  separate `overlaps` dataset that is probably the right long-term shape and
  loses now on size:
  `docs/proposals/comparability-asymmetric-range.md`.
