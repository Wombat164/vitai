- Grade adjusted distance, from Minetti et al. 2002's published cost-of-running
  curve. A hilly 10 km costs what a longer flat one does, and comparing the two
  by distance alone reports the terrain as a statement about the athlete.
  `RouteStats.grade_adjusted` carries the flat-equivalent beside the measured
  distance, never instead of it.
- The PUBLISHED curve, not a vendor's. Strava's grade adjustment is
  undocumented and tuned for competitive parity rather than metabolic cost, so
  a figure derived from it is one nobody can check against a source.
- A gradient steeper than the study measured is REFUSED rather than clamped -
  the polynomial turns sharply outside its range and far enough down goes
  negative, which would say running off a cliff releases energy. Where part of
  a route is beyond the curve, the adjusted figure describes the part that
  could be judged and `covered_pct` says how much that was.
- Absent stays absent: a track with no elevation is not a flat track, and gets
  no adjustment rather than a multiplier of one.
