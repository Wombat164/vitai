- **A persona whose weight series was weighed rather than drawn** (#462).
  `kenji` is a calibration technician running a twenty-five week n-of-1 on
  himself: twelve weeks at a deliberate deficit, thirteen at deliberate
  maintenance, weighing every morning and logging both energy figures every
  day. His weight is not walked. Each morning's mass is carried forward from
  the balance he logged, divided by 7,700 kcal/kg, and only the READING adds
  scatter.
- **The scatter is adopted, not chosen** (G85). Schneditz et al., "Day-to-day
  variability in euvolemic body mass", Ren Fail 2023;45(2):2273421 - 9,521
  days of standardised morning weighing in one healthy individual, SD of the
  relative day-to-day difference 0.53%, rising to 0.69% at seven days. His
  record reproduces both: 0.54% at one day, 0.64% at seven. The four series
  #459 measured run 0.03 to 0.50 kg of seven-day spread; his runs 1.20 kg.
- **The paper reports the SD of a DIFFERENCE, not of a reading**, and the two
  are not the same number. Two independent deviations differ with SD sigma
  root two, so injecting 0.53% as per-reading scatter would produce day-to-day
  movement near 0.75% - forty per cent more than was measured, which is the
  same class of error as being too flat and harder to notice. The generator
  divides by root two and says why.
- **The estimator can now be wrong.** `agreement.compute_agreement` answered
  `explains: false` on all three records it could be asked at all, with
  implied densities of -65,198, -8,469 and +40,307 kcal/kg. A gate that has
  only ever returned one answer has been exercised in one direction. On this
  record it returns true, correlation -0.52, implied density 7,095 kcal/kg
  against the 7,700 it was built with, and the fitted model beats the record's
  own median (1.005 against 1.200). `ANSWERS_YES` is pinned beside
  `ANSWERS_NO` so a change that breaks the arithmetic fails somewhere.
- **It is not evidence about physiology and the register says so twice.** The
  coupling is authored; an estimator that finds it has found something the
  generator put there. What it makes falsifiable is the instrument, not the
  model, and `agreement.py`'s argument for why a centre cannot be stated is
  untouched.
