- `marcus` logs the interval of every night, not just its length. Across the
  ten personas and the demo `sleep_h` was 61.8% populated and `sleep_start`
  and `sleep_end` were 0%, so any design that has to say when a day begins, or
  which part of the day a session fell in, was being settled against an anchor
  nothing could check.
- The interval is derived from his work pattern rather than sampled from
  nothing. He teaches, so the night before a school day runs about an hour
  earlier than the night before a Saturday, a Sunday or a school holiday - his
  term dates were already in the generator. `sleep_end` is `sleep_start` plus
  the `sleep_h` already on the row, to the second, so the two fields describing
  one night cannot disagree.
- Five nights now cross a British Summer Time change, which no fixture had.
  The wall clock moves an hour and the elapsed time does not, so a reader
  subtracting local times is wrong on exactly those rows and one comparing
  instants is not - the naive-versus-aware distinction present as data rather
  than only as a rule.
- The night bounds the day it is dated to: no session and no weigh-in falls
  before that morning's wake. And the generator now has one definition of
  British Summer Time rather than two, which had disagreed on twelve days a
  year - putting a run twelve minutes before a wake by the wall clock and
  forty-eight minutes after it by the instant, on the same morning. Seven
  session timestamps are corrected by that reconciliation.
