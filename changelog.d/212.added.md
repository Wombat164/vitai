- The coarse tier of a measurement's temporal identity: which part of the
  athlete's own day a timed row fell in. `Vitai.phases`, `vitai phases`, and
  the matching agent tool; the rule itself is published in `schema()` so a
  client stops re-deriving it.
- THE ANCHOR IS THE ATHLETE, NOT THE CLOCK. Quarters of the day are
  clock-defined right up until somebody works nights, at which point "morning"
  and 07:00 come apart - and every persona in the corpus but one sleeps at
  night, so a clock-derived phase and a sleep-derived one agree on almost all
  of them. That is the shape that lets a wrong default survive review. Phases
  are hours since waking, which for an ordinary riser reproduces the usual
  reading of the day almost exactly and for a night worker does not.
- A row with no sleep behind it has NO phase, and comes back saying so. It is
  not "probably morning" because most of them are, and the row is not filtered
  out either - a consumer handed only the answerable rows reads an absence as
  a small number. The CLI says how many are unanchored rather than leaving
  dashes to be counted.
- Nothing is stored. The governing decision is that the athlete's own
  timestamps propose and sleep confirms, so an unconfirmed phase is not a fact
  to write into an append-only record; confirming the rest needs a channel to
  ask, which is #224.
- `daily.sleep_end` leaves the written-but-never-read register, because this is
  the reader. `sleep_start` stays: when the athlete went to bed is not what the
  day is anchored on.
- Fixed on the way, and older than this change: validating a `daily` row whose
  sleep boundaries mix a naive stamp with an offset-aware one RAISED. A bare
  `<=` between them threw a TypeError out of `validate_record`, so one such row
  took down every append, every build and `validate` itself rather than
  reporting a single line. Two timestamps that cannot be compared are an
  outcome to report, never an exception.
- A third question kind, `waking` (#224's channel). "The athlete's own
  timestamps propose a waking window" was tried and retracted on this issue -
  a logging habit and a waking window are indistinguishable in the record, so
  there is no honest inference left to make. The engine asks instead:
  `Vitai.questions()` now includes, for each of `weight` and `sessions`, the
  most recent day `phases()` cannot place - one unanchored reading is enough,
  because #212 names a single-row motivation (a narrative claim such as
  "morning weigh-in", which a phase is what makes checkable against a device
  timestamp) alongside its two-row ones, and a rule that waited for a second
  same-day reading before asking silenced that motivation on every record,
  permanently. What the answer supplies today is the ANCHOR that comparison
  needs rather than the comparison itself, since no `weight` or `sessions`
  row can carry a stated phase yet: it is worth asking regardless, because
  the anchor is the part only the athlete holds and the part that gets
  harder to recall every week.
  RECENCY is the volume control, not how many readings a day carries: only
  the day closest to the record's own horizon competes for the question, per
  dataset, so a backlog of any length still asks at most once per dataset.
  Measured against the shipped corpus rather than assumed, both before and
  after this was checked: at most one question per dataset per record either
  way, five in total across thirteen personas and the demo with the
  now-removed extra condition in place, eighteen without it - still capped
  at two per record, because most unanchored days in the corpus are not the
  one closest to the horizon and never win recency for their dataset.
  Answering is an ordinary `daily.sleep_end` append, the same field any other
  waking is recorded with - no new field, no write path, no contract change.
  Nothing is stored on `weight` or `sessions` themselves; the phase still
  only ever arrives from `phases()`, confirmed or not at all.
